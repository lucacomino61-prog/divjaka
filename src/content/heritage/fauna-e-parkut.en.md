Birds are the face of the park, but not its only residents. The bulletin of the National Agency of Protected Areas counts about 25 mammal species and 29 species of reptiles and amphibians; the Ornithological Society gives at least 35 mammal species and more than 1,000 insect species. The figures vary with the source and the year.[^fauna-01]

## Mammals

The mammals recorded include the golden jackal (*Canis aureus*), the Eurasian otter (*Lutra lutra*), badger, stone marten, weasel, polecat, European hare, edible dormouse and several bats; the wolf is listed for the park’s farmland.[^fauna-02] This list comes from a report on the areas taken out of the park in 2022, so these animals live in the park or right next to it.

## Reptiles and frogs

The reptiles include Hermann’s tortoise (*Testudo hermanni*), two pond turtles (*Emys orbicularis* and *Mauremys rivulata*), several snakes and the nose-horned viper.[^fauna-03] The amphibians include the endemic Albanian water frog (*Pelophylax shqipericus*), along with the European tree frog and toads.[^fauna-04]

## Fish of the lagoon

The 1995 Ramsar sheet lists eel, gilthead seabream, European seabass, the killifish *Aphanius fasciatus* and a goby (*Gobius*).[^fauna-05] The main commercial species are eel, grey mullet, gilthead seabream and European seabass.[^lagoon-12]

## How to see them

Keep to the marked paths and keep your distance from the animals. Hunting is forbidden in protected areas[^park-20], and so is driving off public roads and designated places.[^park-21]
