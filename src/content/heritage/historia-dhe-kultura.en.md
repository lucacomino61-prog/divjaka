Divjakë (in its definite form, Divjaka) is a town and municipality in Fier County; the municipality includes the administrative units of Grabian, Gradishtë, Remas and Tërbuf.[^hist-01] The town lies about 5 km from the Adriatic Sea, south-east of Karavasta Lagoon.[^hist-02] The plain it stands on, Myzeqe, takes its name from the medieval Muzaka family; the name is first recorded as “Musachia” in 1417.[^hist-18]

## Ancient traces

Spears, plates and oil lamps of the 3rd–2nd century BC were found in the hills around Divjakë and once kept in the Divjakë museum; a limestone relief of an Erinys with snake-like hair was found in the outer wall of the Divjakë church.[^hist-04] In 2025 the first excavations began at an ancient settlement in the Bishçukas hills, uncovering a basilica, early coins, a granite column and burials from the Hellenistic period to the late Middle Ages.[^hist-05]

Archaeologist Bashkim Lahi identifies a 5–6 ha ancient city on the hill of Babunjë, between Divjakë and Lushnjë, as the Arnisa named by Ptolemy, founded by settlers from Apollonia early in the 6th century BC; other sources date it differently.[^hist-06]

## Karavasta

The name Karavasta appears in documents as early as 1297, when men of the Matarango family “de Caravastasi” robbed a ship anchored in the lagoon.[^lagoon-10] The Church of Saint Athanasius in Karavasta was built in 1778, and the Byzantine-style Church of Saint Nicholas in the village of Xeng dates from 1776.[^hist-08] The “7 Churches of Karavasta”, in the village of Karavasta e Re, date from the 18th century, with simple local architecture and icons showing Byzantine and Orthodox influence.[^hist-09]

## Ardenica

Ardenica Monastery stands on a 237 m hill, 18 km south of Lushnjë, about 35 km (about 40 minutes) by road from Divjakë.[^hist-13] Its founding date is uncertain: the National Tourism Agency says the 13th century, English Wikipedia 1282, Albanian Wikipedia around 1600.[^hist-15] Skanderbeg married Andronika Arianiti here on 21 April 1451.[^hist-14]

## Apollonia and Byllis

Apollonia, near the village of Pojan, was founded around 600 BC by Greek colonists from Corinth and later flourished as a Roman city where the young Augustus studied; it is about 52 km (about 55 minutes) by road from Divjakë.[^hist-16] Byllis, near Hekal, was the chief city of the Illyrian Bylliones; its walls were built around 350 BC. It is about 79 km away, about 1 h 30 min by road.[^hist-17]

## At the table

Divjakë’s cooking mixes sea and land: fish and seafood from the lagoon and the Adriatic (eel, sea bass, shrimp), grilled or stewed with local vegetables, and milk and cheese from nearby farms.[^hist-21] The park area produces cereals, vegetables and fruit, olive oil, wine and honey.[^hist-22] Divjakë is also known as Albania’s largest watermelon-growing area.[^hist-23]

## Celebrations

Dalmatian Pelican Day was proposed in 2017 and is celebrated around 10 May; on 11 May 2019 the park hosted a celebration with a fair of local products.[^hist-20]
