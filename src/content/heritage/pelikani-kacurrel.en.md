The Dalmatian pelican (*Pelecanus crispus*) is the emblem of Divjakë-Karavasta National Park, and the park is the only place in Albania where it breeds.[^birds-01] It is the largest pelican: its body is 1.6–1.8 m long, its wings usually span 2.7–3.2 m and it weighs about 9.5 kg on average. It is the largest bird species in Albania.[^birds-13]

## Why “curly”

In the breeding season the adults’ bill pouch turns from orange to bright red, and curly feathers grow on the head. The Albanian name, *kaçurrel* (“curly”), comes from them.[^birds-11]

## The island

The colony nests on Pelican Island, a small islet in the lagoon about 0.5 m above the water, covered in reeds and herbs.[^lagoon-13] The pelicans build their nests from January and stay until June, when the breeding season ends.[^birds-10] The island is closed to the public: visitors watch it from the observation towers and from boat trips nearby.[^park-23]

## A colony that came back

The colony once held 200–250 breeding pairs. The sources disagree on how long that lasted; numbers then fell sharply through disturbance, egg theft, illegal killing, habitat change and a lack of fish.[^birds-03] At the lowest point, in the early 2000s, 19 pairs were left.[^birds-04]

In 2019, 68 pairs nested, the highest figure in 30 years, while for about six months the park administration and the Ornithological Society’s wardens guarded the colony around the clock.[^birds-06] In 2020 exactly 85 pairs were counted in three sub-colonies, raising about 65 young that could fly.[^birds-07] We found no official count after 2020; some sources speak of about 100 pairs without giving a year.[^birds-08]

## Status

On the IUCN Red List the Dalmatian pelican is Near Threatened (NT), as assessed on 1 October 2017.[^birds-12]

## Five pelicans with trackers

In June 2024, shortly before they could fly, five young pelicans hatched that year were fitted with satellite trackers and named Adriatik, Arnisa, Bazhorr, Godulla and Artir. On 7 August 2024 Adriatik was at Great Prespa Lake, three others were on Portëz beach at Cape Bisht-Pallë, and Godulla stayed near Divjakë.[^birds-09]

## The pelicans at the Visitor Centre

In 2021 two pelicans lived at Albania’s first bird rehabilitation centre, inside the park: Johnny, who could not fly, and Vlashi.[^birds-27] The pelicans in the photo below, outside the Visitor Centre, are tame birds, not part of the wild colony.

## How to watch without disturbing them

The best time for the pelicans is the breeding season, January to June; guides suggest early morning, when the water is calm and the light is good.[^prac-13] Dalmatian Pelican Day is celebrated in the park around 10 May.[^hist-20]
