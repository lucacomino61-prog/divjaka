The lagoon and the Divjaka forest have been protected for more than half a century. The study by Colacino and Marka (2009) gives the year 1966; EcoAlbania writes 1964.[^park-10] Today the park is a Category II protected area, a national park.[^park-08]

## Step by step

- **1994.** Decision No. 413 makes the “Pisha Divjake – Lagoon of Karavasta” ecological zone a specially protected zone, to be included in the Ramsar Convention.[^park-09]
- **1995.** The Karavasta Lagoon ecosystem becomes Ramsar site no. 781, with a designation date of 31 October 1995 and 20,000 ha in the Ramsar register; other sources give other dates.[^park-14] It was Albania’s first Ramsar site.[^park-15]
- **2007.** Decision No. 687 declares a national park with an enlarged area of 22,230.24 ha, from the Shkumbin mouth in the north to the Seman mouth in the south.[^park-01][^park-02]
- **2015.** The park’s management plan is approved, prepared in a project funded by the Japanese Government through JICA.[^park-13]
- **2017.** A Mabetex Group plan for a large resort inside the park (about 1,170 ha, 2,400 apartments, 370 villas) is opposed by environmental organisations and blocked.[^hist-12]
- **2022.** The government revises the boundaries. The decision text puts the area at 22,389.08 ha, 34.7% of it sea.[^park-05] According to the Ornithological Society, 1,751 ha of farmland and built-up land were removed and 2,308 ha of sea and shore were added; the two sources’ figures do not fully match.[^park-06] Eleven environmental organisations took the decision to court; the first hearing was held on 10 March 2023.[^park-07]

## What makes it special

The lagoon is an Important Bird Area (IBA AL006), and the wider site is a Key Biodiversity Area.[^park-16] It is also a candidate for the Bern Convention’s Emerald Network.[^park-17] The park is managed by the National Agency of Protected Areas through its regional administration in Fier.[^park-11]

## Rules for visitors

Law No. 81/2017 “On protected areas” sets rules that apply to you too:

- Hunting is forbidden in every protected area.[^park-20]
- Do not drive or park off public roads and designated places; do not collect plants in quantity; do not camp or light fires outside designated points and trails.[^park-21]
- In the recreation zone, do not leave waste; in the strictest parts of the core zone, boats, motor vehicles and fires are forbidden.[^park-22]
- Pelican Island is closed to the public; it can only be watched from the towers and from boats nearby.[^park-23]
- Some trails may be closed in the breeding season, and the closure is not always signed at the start of the trail.[^park-24]

## Getting in

The Ministry of Environment has announced controlled access with electronic ticketing on the park road, with a 50-lek ticket for tourists only; local residents are exempt. We have not verified whether the ticket is collected today.[^park-19] The road with the barrier is the only one leading to the park and to Divjaka beach.[^coast-04]

## Visitors

According to an Associated Press report, visitor numbers rose from about 1,300 in 2014 to about 550,000 in 2019, and halved in 2020 during the pandemic.[^park-18]
