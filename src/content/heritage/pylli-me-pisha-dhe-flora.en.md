The Divjaka coastal pine forest is the only natural coastal pine forest in Albania. It runs along Divjaka beach and has paths for walking and cycling.[^flora-02] From the shade of the pines you reach the sea in a few minutes.[^coast-03]

## Two pines

This is a native forest of stone pine (*Pinus pinea*), the only one of its kind on the Albanian coast. Aleppo pine (*Pinus halepensis*) was brought from Italy early in the 20th century and has made parts of the forest semi-natural.[^flora-01] The sources do not fully agree: the Key Biodiversity Area factsheet also names *Pinus pinaster*.[^lagoon-07]

How big is the forest? The 1994 decision records 1,162 ha; other sources give anything from about 1,100 to 1,250 ha.[^flora-03]

## Under the pines

Stone pine grows with myrtle (*Myrtus communis*), oriental hornbeam (*Carpinus orientalis*) and tree heath (*Erica arborea*); Aleppo pine with juniper, *Phillyrea angustifolia* and other shrubs.[^flora-05] Pedunculate oak (*Quercus robur*), field maple and narrow-leaved ash have also been recorded in the park.[^flora-06]

## Plants found only here

The endemic plants include *Aster albanicus* subsp. *paparistoi* and the Albanian orchid (*Orchis albanica*), which flowers in spring in the sunny, open parts of the forest.[^flora-07]

## Dunes and salty ground

On the dunes grow large-fruited juniper, sea daffodil (*Pancratium maritimum*) and *Cyperus kalli*; further inland, pines with mastic and myrtle, while brackish patches hold *Juncus acutus*, *Schoenus nigricans* and *Saccharum ravennae*.[^flora-09] The EU-protected habitats found here include coastal lagoons, coastal dunes with juniper and wooded dunes with pine.[^flora-10]

## Keep it safe from fire

Summer is the hardest time for fires in the park; many start when farmers burn fields close to the forest.[^prac-10] In the sustainable-use sub-zones, camping and lighting fires outside designated points are forbidden, and so is collecting plants on a large scale.[^park-21]
